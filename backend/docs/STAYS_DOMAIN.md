# The stays domain (Phase 4)

**Status (Phase 5 added):** the data model, the billing engine **and the stay lifecycle** exist and are tested. A paid booking
creates its stay (see "Phase 5: the lifecycle" at the end); two endpoints exist (vacate request, end). Reading stays and dues
is Phase 7. This page is what Phases 6 to 8 may rely on.

## The words

- **Stay**: a student's live tenancy in one bed, created from a paid booking. One stay per booking.
- **Due**: one billing cycle of a stay. A booking pays **Due 1** (kind `BOOKING_PERIOD`, the period starting on the join
  date) and **Due 2** (kind `RENT`, the first rent) is scheduled.
- **Period**: the dates a due covers, first day to last day inclusive. A period is due on its **first day**.
- **Outbox**: a table of work to do outside a request (reminders and the like). Only the table exists; the consumer is Phase 8.

## The two billing rules (`apps/stays/billing.py`, pure Python)

| Rule | When | How the dates work |
|---|---|---|
| **ANNIVERSARY** (default) | every hostel unless configured otherwise | Join on the 18th, pay on the 18th of each following month. `due(n) = anchor + n months`, day = `min(anchor day, last day of that month)`, **always computed from the anchor, never from the previous due**. 31 Jan gives 28 Feb (29 in a leap year), 31 Mar, 30 Apr, 31 May. |
| **FIXED_DAY** (rare, set per hostel) | the owner wants rent on one day for everyone | Due on a fixed day 1 to 28, or the last day of the month. The first period runs from the join date to the next fixed due day, charged by the first-period policy. |

First-period policy for FIXED_DAY (decision D1): **FULL_MONTH** charges a whole month's rent for the first (shorter) period;
**PRORATE** charges only the part of the fixed period that is left.

**Joining exactly on the fixed due day** starts a new period, so the first charge is a whole period for either policy (the
"next" due day is always strictly after the join date).

## The one rule still waiting for the founder (`prorated_amount`)

```
prorated first rent = monthly rent x (days from the join date to the next fixed due day)
                      / (days in the fixed period, previous fixed due day to next fixed due day, that contains the join date)
```

Rounded to a **whole rupee**, halves rounded up, with `Decimal`: exactly the board's integer formula
`floor((2 x rent x first + full) / (2 x full))`, where `first` is the days to the next fixed due day and `full` the days in
that fixed period. Worked example (the AdminBilling board): rent 6,000, fixed day 1, joining 18 September. The fixed period is
1 Sep to 1 Oct (30 days); 18 Sep to 1 Oct is 13 days; 6,000 x 13 / 30 = **2,600**, next due 1 Oct 2026.
It lives in **one function** (`prorated_amount`), so a different rule changes one place. **Please confirm or name another
rule** (`FOUNDER_DEFERRED.md`, item 25). Until then the default policy for a new config is FULL_MONTH, which does not use it.

## How it was checked against the approved screens

The Phase 4 brief says the AdminBilling board is the source of truth for the billing maths. Its own script (the preview) was
run in Node for the four sample joins (18 Sep 2026, 31 Jan 2027, 1 Sep 2026, 5 Sep 2026) in ANNIVERSARY and in FIXED_DAY with
each due day (1, 5, 10, 15, last day) and both policies: **44 cases, rent 6,000**. The expected rows (period, due date, amount
for the booking and four rents) are saved in `apps/stays/test_data/adminbilling_golden.json`, generated by the board's script
and not by the engine, and `test_billing.py` requires the engine to reproduce every row exactly, plus the "13 of 30 days"
counts the board's summary line shows (`fixed_period_days`). **The UI pack folder `docs/ui` named in the brief is not in the
repository** (KNOWN_GAPS G4-02): the board was read from `HH Stay and Rent Screens.html` in the founder's Downloads folder.

## The engine's promises

- No database, no Django, no other app, and **no clock**: `today` and grace days are arguments (a test scans the source).
- Money is `Decimal` rupees in whole paise; a float is refused. The proration result is a whole rupee (stored with two places, 2600.00). Dates are plain `date`s, never `datetime`s.
- `build_periods(join_date, mode, policy, rent, fixed_due_day, count)` returns periods with no gap and no overlap; Phase 5
  takes the first two as Due 1 and Due 2, and Phase 8 takes later ones.
- `effective_status(stored, today, due, window_days, grace_days)`: **overdue is never stored** (E8); it is worked out from the
  dates. Payable opens `payable_window_days` before the due date (default 7); overdue starts after the due date plus
  `grace_days` (default 0).
- All dates a person sees are **IST** (`apps/stays/dates.py: today_ist()`); midnight IST is 18:30 UTC the day before.

## The models (`apps/stays/models.py`)

| Model | Table | Scope | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `HostelBillingConfig` | `hostel_billing_configs` | owner (`hostel__owner`) | One per hostel; **no row means the defaults** (`HostelBillingConfig.effective_for(hostel)`). The database refuses a fixed day outside 1 to 28, a fixed-day hostel with no day, both a day and "last day", and a day on an anniversary hostel. |
| `Stay` | `stays` | owner and student | The vacate request is `vacate_requested_at`, `planned_move_out_date` and `vacate_reason` (Course completed, Moving city, Found another place, Other: the wording of the 'Request to vacate' board). Optional link to an existing `Resident` (never created automatically, E5). |
| `Due` | `dues` | owner and student (through the stay) | Unique on (stay, period_start); a period cannot end before it starts; an amount cannot be negative; indexes on (status, due_date) and (stay, due_date). | `OutboxEvent` | `outbox_events` | none (internal) | Unique `dedupe_key`, so writing an event twice is harmless. Payloads hold **ids and codes only, never personal data**. Index on (status, next_attempt_at) for the consumer's query. |

The migration (`stays.0001_initial`) only creates the four tables, three indexes and three constraints on them; it adds nothing
to an existing table and reverses by dropping them (checked forwards, backwards and forwards again). Tenancy: `stays` is in the
explicit app list (`apps/core/tenancy/checks.py`) and the startup check passes.

## Decisions made in this phase (please review)

1. **A stay's student is optional** (null, set to null if the account is deleted), like `Booking.student`: a booking marked paid
   offline can have no account. Phase 5 decides whether such a booking gets a stay.
2. **No database rule limits a student to one current stay.** Decision D2 is a booking-time check in a later phase, and E4 says a
   second paid booking must never be refused after the money is taken; that stay is created and flagged `needs_review`.
3. **Default first-period policy is FULL_MONTH**, because the proration rule is unconfirmed.
4. **Rent must be in whole paise** (two decimal places at most) or the engine refuses it, rather than rounding silently. (Only the proration result is rounded, to a whole rupee, as the board does.)
5. **Table names** follow the repository's style: `stays`, `dues`, `outbox_events`, `hostel_billing_configs`.
6. **The admin cannot type over money or add stays**: dues' amount, status, payment and kind, and a stay's rent and snapshot, are
   read-only; stays, dues and outbox events cannot be added by hand (code creates them). This avoids the problem of
   KNOWN_GAPS G1-07 (a booking made paid by hand) for the new tables.
7. **Limits on the config**: payable window 0 to 60 days, grace 0 to 30 days, enforced by the database too.
8. **The fixed due day is passed to the engine as a whole number 1 to 28 or the marker `LAST_DAY`**; the model stores it as a
   nullable day plus a last-day flag, as specified.

## What Phase 5 should do with this

## Phase 5: the lifecycle (`apps/stays/services.py`)

**What the first payment covers (confirmed in `STAY_BASELINE.md` A8):** one month's rent at `Room.price_per_month`, nothing else.
So Due 1 (`BOOKING_PERIOD`) is that first period, `PAID`, linked to the payment; Due 2 is the next period.

| Piece | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `create_stay_for_booking(booking, payment)` | Idempotent (one stay per booking). Takes the booking row lock, so two callers at once make one stay. Needs a PAID booking; rent is the room price (or, with no room, the successful payment's amount), never `Booking.amount`. Writes Stay, Due 1 (PAID, `paid_at` = the booking's paid time), Due 2 (PAYABLE inside the window, else SCHEDULED), links a Resident only if exactly one has `linked_booking` = this booking, and one outbox row `stay.created` (dedupe `stay-created-<booking id>`). Join date = `check_in_date`, else the paid date in IST. Status UPCOMING if the join date is in the future, else ACTIVE. |
| `ensure_stay_for_paid_booking` | What the paid paths call, inside their own transaction, in a **savepoint**. Any failure is logged (`hh.stays`, which Sentry sees) and swallowed: **the payment is never rolled back** because a stay could not be built. `reconcile_stays` heals it later. |
| The three paid paths | `confirm_payment` (card callback and webhook), `BookingViewSet.verify` (owner), `BookingViewSet.mark_paid` (offline). `verify` now has the missing status guard (G1-02). Both owner actions take the bed first and the booking row second, the payment code's lock order. |
| D2 guard | `BookingCreateSerializer.validate`: a signed-in student with a stay in UPCOMING, ACTIVE or NOTICE gets **409** `{code: ACTIVE_STAY_EXISTS, message, current_stay: {id, hostel_name, room_label, bed_label, paid_until}}` (the BookingBlocked board). Anonymous bookings are allowed. A second PAID booking is never refused: its stay is created with `needs_review = True`. |
| `request_vacate` | Student, own stay, ACTIVE to NOTICE; date today (IST) or later; reason one of Course completed, Moving city, Found another place, Other; one outbox row `stay.vacate_requested`. A second request is refused (409). |
| `end_stay` | Owner of the hostel, or an admin (`is_staff`). ENDED, `ended_at`, **future** unpaid dues cancelled (a cycle that already started stays payable), the bed released (`Bed.is_available = True`, locked first; it stays taken if another current stay holds the same bed). The booking stays `paid` (history). Ending an ended stay is a no-op. |
| `reconcile_stays [--dry-run] [--batch-size N]` | Creates the missing stay for paid bookings (oldest first, at most N per run, resumable by running again); prints counts only. **Read KNOWN_GAPS G5-03 before the first real run.** |

Endpoints: `POST /api/v1/stays/<id>/vacate-request/` (the stay's student) and `POST /api/v1/stays/<id>/end/` (owner or admin). Anyone
else gets 404 (not 403), so a stay's existence is not revealed.

**Lock order:** Payment, Bed, Booking (as in `holds.py`). `end_stay` locks the Bed, then the Stay.

## Phase 6: rent payments

| Piece | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `Payment` additions (migration `payments.0006`, additive, every column defaulted in the database) | `purpose` (BOOKING default, RENT), `due` (null FK to `stays.Due`), `source` (ONLINE default, OFFLINE_OWNER), `recorded_by`, `reference_note`, `paid_via`, and for a void `voided_at`, `voided_by`, `void_reason`. Status gains VOIDED. `receipt_number` is a property (`HH-<date>-<6 characters of the id>`), not a column. A RENT payment keeps `booking` = the stay's booking (the column is required and the payment screens look payments up through it). |
| `POST /payments/create-rent-order/` `{due_id}` | Only the stay's student (404 for anyone else). Due must be payable now (inside the window) or overdue; PAID, WAIVED and CANCELLED give 409 `DUE_PAID` and so on, a due not yet payable gives 409 `DUE_NOT_PAYABLE`. Amount is the due's own amount. Same reply as create-order. A repeat call returns the open order (200). No bed is held. |
| `confirm_payment` RENT branch | Lock order Payment, then Due. Due becomes PAID with `paid_at` and the payment link; one outbox row `rent.paid` (dedupe `rent-paid-<payment id>`). A due already paid, cancelled or waived flags the payment `needs_review` (money to refund by a person). Never touches the booking, bed or stay. The booking branch is unchanged. |
| Holds | `expire_if_lapsed`, `expire_stale`, `peek_hold_has_lapsed` and the `cleanup_expired` holds step skip RENT payments (E7). `payment.failed` closes a rent payment and releases no bed. |
| Webhook method | Read from the Razorpay payment entity: upi, card, netbanking, wallet, anything else is OTHER; an entity with no method is still recorded as CARD (the old behaviour). |
| `POST /stays/dues/<id>/record-payment/` | Owner of the hostel or an admin. `paid_at` (date and time, not in the future), `method` CASH, BANK_TRANSFER or UPI_DIRECT, a required `reference_note` (200 characters). Creates a SUCCESS payment (source OFFLINE_OWNER, `recorded_by`, a synthetic `offline_<random>` order id because the column is required and unique), marks the due PAID and writes the same `rent.paid` row. One record per due: a second call is 409. |
| `POST /stays/dues/<id>/void-payment/` | Same people. Only for an OFFLINE_OWNER rent payment, with a required reason. The payment is kept and marked VOIDED (who, when, why); the due is open again (PAYABLE or SCHEDULED by the window, overdue by date); one outbox row `rent.payment_voided`. A second void is 409. An online payment answers 409 `ONLINE_PAYMENT_NEEDS_REFUND`. |
| Status poll | `GET /payments/status/<id>/` now also returns `purpose` and `due_id`. The payment lists return `purpose`. |

**Lock order for rent:** Payment, then Due (record-payment locks the Due only; void locks Payment then Due). Free-text notes and reasons stay on the
payment row; outbox payloads carry ids and codes only.

## Phase 7: read APIs (student, GET only)

Fixed query counts (measured on the scratch Postgres at 2, 6 and 26 dues): list 3, detail 2, dues 2, payments 3, receipt 3,
analytics 3, calendar 2, home 2 (the cache hit makes it 0 plus the cache read). The stay with its hostel, room and student is one
query; all its dues (with the payment, and the successful attempt where a payment is shown) are prefetched once; the rest is Python.

| Endpoint | Notes |
|---|---|
| `GET /stays/` | Current stay first, then past, paginated like `bookings/my/` (page size 10, at most 100). `openable` is false for ENDED and CANCELLED; their own pages answer 403 `STAY_NOT_OPENABLE`. |
| `GET /stays/home/` | One call for the Home screen. With `STAYS_ENABLED` off: `{"enabled": false}` and nothing else. | `GET /stays/<id>/`, `/dues/`, `/payments/?filter=`, `/receipts/<payment_id>/`, `/analytics/?range=`, `GET /stays/calendar/?month=` | Shapes and wording are mapped board by board in `docs/UI_DATA_MAP.md`. |

## Phase 8: the engine

`stays_daily` (03:30 UTC) activates stays, creates the next dues, queues the reminder ladder; `process_outbox` (every 5 minutes) sends notices and reminders; `apps/notifications` holds the
inbox, the per-channel log, the preferences and the job-run record. Everything is in `backend/docs/ENGINE.md`. Facts other phases rely on:

- Dues now exist ahead of the payable window (the next one is created when the latest is paid or its window opens), so there is something to pay after Due 2 (closes G7-01 for the daily job; reads still project further ahead).
- `OutboxEvent.priority` (higher first) and the index `(status, -priority, next_attempt_at)` keep the claim fast on a big backlog.
- Events written by payments and stays (`stay.created`, `rent.paid`, `stay.vacate_requested`) are now consumed: the student gets an inbox notice, the owner a payment, new-stay or vacate notice.
